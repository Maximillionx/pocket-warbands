# Roadmap: making the core more fun and strategic

## Why the core isn't gripping yet

The game offers a lot of choices but few real decisions. A decision feels strategic when it involves a trade-off, you have enough information to reason about it, and you can see the consequence. Right now:

- **Setup is a stat check.** Rear, line or vanguard rarely changes the result. The enemy lineup is a line of text, not something you plan against.
- **Battles are watched, not played.** The three commander abilities are cooldown buttons with one obvious use. The test bots can't beat bosses without them, so they're mandatory rather than choices.
- **Builds don't combine.** Most traits, rings and gear are stat bumps, so a level 7 army plays like a bigger level 3 army. Formations are the only synergy, and they're half hidden.
- **The economy has no tension.** You spend credits when you have them. There's nothing to save for, no reroll and no sell decision.
- **Two games are splitting focus.** The campaign and Outpost Defense don't feed each other.

Thronefall works because every coin is a visible trade-off (a wall here or a tower there), you can see where the next attack comes from, and you're on the field fixing your own mistakes.

## Three ways to pivot

| Pivot | Core loop | Strategic hook | Effort | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Outpost campaign** | Build by day, defend by night, with your named soldiers as the garrison | Where you build matters, and you can see what's coming | High: needs many maps and buildings | Close to Thronefall, could feel derivative |
| **B. Deep auto-battler** | Shop, synergies and a grid board, like TFT or Super Auto Pets | Building around team bonuses, positioning, economy | Medium: reuses the battle sim | Crowded genre, so the depth has to be real |
| **C. Plan and command** | Read the enemy's telegraphed moves, then make 2 or 3 calls per fight | Foresight puzzles, like Into the Breach | Medium | Slower pace on a phone |

## Recommendation: B plus a light C, then use Outpost to test A

The named warband (nemeses, legends, permanent losses) is what's unique here, so keep it at the heart and give it depth. In priority order:

1. **Show the enemy board and counter tags.** Scouting places the enemy squad on the board. Enemies carry tags (armored, swarm, flying, shielded) and your classes visibly counter them, for example Lancers pierce armor and Gunners shred swarms. Placement becomes your answer to what you see.
2. **Add a synergy bar.** Give each class two tags, with bonuses that unlock at 2, 4 and 6 units sharing a tag. The bar updates live while you drag units, so every recruit becomes a choice about whether to switch builds. Rings become emblems that add a tag, which reuses that system instead of piling more stats on top.
3. **Move to a 4x3 grid with neighbour effects.** A Medic heals the units next to it, a Bulwark guards the unit behind it, a Hype Droid buffs its row. Dragging already works.
4. **Give the economy some tension.** Interest on saved credits, a paid reroll, selling for half, and a capped bench.
5. **Replace the spells with orders.** One or two calls per fight: Focus target, Hold the line, Charge. Bosses telegraph their big move 3 seconds ahead, so each call has a right answer you can read.
6. **Make foresight a resource.** Battles are deterministic, so a scarce "Scan" could show the predicted result of your current setup. Setup turns into a puzzle without becoming solved.
7. **Make Outpost the finale of each sector,** defended by your real roster with real losses. That tests pivot A cheaply. If you keep playing those fights more than the auto-battles, pivot A is the answer.

To keep it "less is more", cut the gear slot or fold it into synergies, and leave paint and visors as cosmetics only.

## How to tell it's working

Build steps 1 to 3 for a single sector and play 10 runs. If you rearrange your board before most fights, the depth is real. If you don't, more content won't fix it.
